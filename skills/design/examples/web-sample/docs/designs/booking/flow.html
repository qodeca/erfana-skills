Choose a session and a time

A patient picks a session, then a week, a day, and a time. Desktop is the main view. Judge whether the time is always the first thing read, and whether the one coral strip marks the right thing.

Open questions
  • Assumed: desktop web is the main view, because most patients book from a computer at home or at work. This replaces the earlier guess that most use a phone, and it is inferred. To confirm from the clinic's visitor numbers.
  • Assumed: a tablet is touch only. A tablet with a keyboard or mouse would need the desktop hover and focus states.
  • Assumed: the two tablet sizes are one 768 by 1024 screen, upright and on its side. Widths between the drawn sizes take the layout below them, and a phone on its side, which is short rather than narrow, is not drawn.
  • Assumed: the page stops growing at 1280 wide and centres in a wider window. A wider screen is not drawn.
  • Assumed: the bottom of a phone screen is where a patient in pain reaches most easily. To test with patients, because the sources on thumb reach are general.
  • Assumed: a patient picks a first visit or a follow-up before choosing a time, with a first visit at 60 minutes and a follow-up at 45. To confirm with the clinic.
  • Assumed: the help wording on Choose a session. To confirm with the clinic.
  • Assumed: any free therapist is fine, so the name sits beside the time and there is no therapist filter. To confirm.
  • Assumed: online booking opens 6 weeks ahead. The number is a guess. The clinic decides.
  • Assumed: the clinic is closed at weekends, and the number of free times on each day is known when the week loads.
  • Assumed: English only, 24-hour times. Other languages are not drawn; labels wrap rather than clip.
Sizes

How this adapts: Each state is drawn desktop, tablet, tablet landscape, then phone. By width, the picker has four columns on a desktop, three panes on a landscape tablet, two on an upright tablet, and one on a phone, where the week becomes seven cells and the actions move to the bottom. Choose a session sets two option panels and a help pane differently at each size.

  • A tablet held upright is drawn as touch only, so it has no hover or keyboard focus state. Its layout is in the state choose.
  • A tablet on its side is drawn as touch only, so it has no hover or keyboard focus state. Its layout is in the state choose.
  • A phone is drawn as touch only, so it has no hover or keyboard focus state. Its layout is in the state choose.
