Accept a shift

A colleague, usually opening a notification, sees one offered shift, whether they can take it, and what happens next. This is the step the baton waits on. Each state is drawn for phone, then tablet, then tablet landscape, and dark frames follow in the same group. Sizes with nothing new are listed under Sizes, with the reason. Frames show the layout iOS and Android share. Where the two differ, each is drawn as its own labelled state under iOS and Android differences.

Open questions
  • Assumed: the colleague sees the note and who offered the shift, but no reason beyond what the note says. To confirm with HR.
  • Assumed: the colleague’s own checks are shown before they accept, using the same rules as the manager’s. To confirm with nursing management.
  • Assumed: saying no needs no reason. To confirm with the ward team.
  • Assumed: a colleague who accepted cannot withdraw in the app and calls the manager instead. To confirm with nursing managers.
  • Assumed: a colleague who accepted is told when a manager declines, but not the manager’s reason, which goes only to the nurse who offered. To confirm with HR.
  • Assumed: a person who said no to an offer to everyone is not told when it is taken. To confirm.
  • Not designed: the notification itself. This page starts where it opens.
  • Names, wards, and hours are invented. The longest real name on staff should replace them.
  • Assumed: a colleague opens one offer at a time on a tablet too, so there is no list of offers. If offers get a list (open decision 8), the first column becomes that list. To confirm with the product owner.
  • Assumed: on a shared ward tablet, the person answering is the person signed in. Another nurse tapping Accept shift by mistake is not designed for. To confirm with the ward team and IT.
  • Assumed: a tablet offers Message and shows the number, because many tablets cannot call. Which messaging app it opens, and whether a tablet that can call should still call, are open. To confirm with IT.
  • Assumed: rotating a tablet mid-task keeps the open item, any typed text, and any open sheet. To confirm with engineering.
  • Assumed: offers close 24 hours before the shift starts. To confirm with nursing management.
  • Assumed: the rest rule is 11 hours between shifts. Local law and union agreements may differ.
  • Assumed: a nurse may offer to one person or to everyone who can take it, and the first to accept takes it. To confirm with the ward team.
  • Assumed: two panes at 768, though Android’s guide shows one pane at this width. To confirm on a real tablet.
  • Assumed: touch only on tablets, with no pointer, so no hover is drawn. A paired keyboard or trackpad on an iPad may need one. To confirm with the ward team.
  • Assumed: the action bar under the main pane is reachable when a tablet is propped in landscape or held in two hands. Not tested. To confirm on a device.

How this screen adapts

SizeLayoutMain action
PhoneEverything in one columnAt the bottom, in reach of one thumb
TabletTwo columns, the shift and who offered it, then the checks. What happens next is under the checksA bar under the main pane, with the main button at the inline end. A step that leads to a commit stays out of that commit’s slot
Tablet landscapeThree columns: the shift, the checks, and what happens nextA bar under the main pane, with the main button at the inline end. A step that leads to a commit stays out of that commit’s slot

On a phone the action bar is drawn at the end of the content, because a review page hides nothing. In the product it is pinned to the bottom of the screen.
