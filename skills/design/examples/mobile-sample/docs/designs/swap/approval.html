Swap requests

A ward manager, between other work, decides one request in under a minute. Requests arrive only after a colleague has accepted. Each state is drawn for phone, then tablet, then tablet landscape, and dark frames follow in the same group. Sizes with nothing new are listed under Sizes, with the reason. Frames show the layout iOS and Android share. Where the two differ, each is drawn as its own labelled state under iOS and Android differences.

Open questions
  • Assumed: a manager reaches Swap requests from the new-request notification or the app's main menu. That entry is not drawn here. To confirm with the hospital.
  • Assumed: the ward manager approves, and a stand-in manager can approve when they are away. To confirm who decides on nights and weekends.
  • Assumed: over weekly hours and too little rest both stop a swap, and no manager can override either. Whether a manager may override hours stays out until HR and the union decide.
  • Assumed: the rest rule is 11 hours between shifts. Local law and union agreements may differ.
  • Assumed: a manager must give a reason to decline, and the nurse sees it word for word. To confirm.
  • Assumed: managers approve on the phone, between other work. To confirm by watching one do it.
  • Names, wards, and hours are invented. The longest real name on staff should replace them.
  • Assumed: managers use a tablet in the ward office or at the station, in landscape, and this list and detail side by side is where they spend the most time. To confirm by watching one do it.
  • Assumed: the soonest request opens on its own on a tablet, because a manager’s next step is always the most urgent one. To confirm with a manager.
  • Assumed: a shared ward tablet does not stay signed in as a manager. Who can approve from a shared screen is not drawn. To confirm with nursing management and IT.
  • Assumed: a tablet offers a message to the nursing office and shows the number, because many tablets cannot call. To confirm with IT.
  • Assumed: on a tablet the keyboard pushes the decline sheet up rather than covering it, and its height comes from the system. To confirm on a device.
  • Assumed: rotating a tablet mid-task keeps the open item, any typed text, and any open sheet. To confirm with engineering.
  • Assumed: two panes at 768, though Android’s guide shows one pane at this width. To confirm on a real tablet.
  • Assumed: touch only on tablets, with no pointer, so no hover is drawn. A paired keyboard or trackpad on an iPad may need one. To confirm with the ward team.
  • Assumed: the action bar under the main pane is reachable when a tablet is propped in landscape or held in two hands. Not tested. To confirm on a device.
  • Not confirmed: Android’s sheet limit of 640. No page read gave the number. To confirm with engineering.
  • Decided here, reversible: a tap outside the decline sheet does nothing once a reason is typed, and works like Keep request when the field is empty. Keep request with a typed reason discards it. To confirm with a manager.
  • Decided here, reversible: Approve and Decline are off while a tablet list updates. To confirm with a manager.
  • Decided here, reversible: holds last half a second at least. They are Approve swap after a sheet closes or a request opens, Send offer on a reopened draft, a decline sheet’s buttons as the keyboard moves it, and a commit at 200% text. The length is a stand-in. To confirm on a device.
Sizes
  • Every state on this page is drawn at every size, because a decision here must never rest on an undrawn layout.

How this screen adapts

SizeLayoutMain action
PhoneThe list, then one request on its own screenAt the bottom, in reach of one thumb
TabletRequests beside the open one, with its checks under itA bar under the main pane, with the main button at the inline end. A step that leads to a commit stays out of that commit’s slot
Tablet landscapeRequests, the open one, and its checksA bar under the main pane, with the main button at the inline end. A step that leads to a commit stays out of that commit’s slot

On a phone the action bar is drawn at the end of the content, because a review page hides nothing. In the product it is pinned to the bottom of the screen.

On a tablet the soonest request opens on its own, and the next opens after a decision only when the manager taps Next request. The list is beside the request, so Back to requests is phone only.
