Offer a shift

A nurse, often tired and between tasks, picks a shift, chooses who gets the offer, and sends it. Each state is drawn for phone, then tablet, then tablet landscape, and dark frames follow in the same group. Sizes with nothing new are listed under Sizes, with the reason. Frames show the layout iOS and Android share. Where the two differ, each is drawn as its own labelled state on iOS and Android differences.

Open questions
  • Assumed: offers close 24 hours before the shift starts. To confirm with nursing management.
  • Assumed: the rest rule is 11 hours between shifts. Local law and union agreements may differ.
  • Assumed: over weekly hours and too little rest both rule a colleague out, and no manager can override either. Whether a manager may override hours stays out until HR and the union decide.
  • Not seen: real Android type. Review pages ship no font, so without Roboto installed a Mac shows Android frames in a fallback. Check Android type on a device.
  • Assumed: colleagues see only a general reason such as "Needs more rest", never leave or health details. To confirm with HR.
  • Assumed: a nurse may offer to one person or to everyone who can take it, and the first to accept takes it. To confirm with the ward team.
  • Assumed: the nurse stays on the shift until the manager approves. To confirm with nursing management.
  • Names, wards, and hours are invented. The longest real name on staff should replace them.
  • Assumed: a nurse also uses this on a tablet of their own at home or on a break, held in two hands. To confirm by asking nurses.
  • Assumed: the same screens run on a shared ward tablet at the nurses’ station, propped in landscape. Sign-in, switching person, and locking the screen on a shared tablet are not drawn. To confirm with the ward team and IT.
  • Assumed: seeing a colleague’s shifts and “Needs more rest” on a shared screen is acceptable. It may not be, because others can read over a shoulder. To confirm with HR.
  • Assumed: nothing opens on its own when the list first shows, because the shift is the nurse’s own choice. To confirm with a nurse.
  • Assumed: two panes at 768, though Android’s guide shows one pane at this width. To confirm on a real tablet.
  • Assumed: touch only on tablets, with no pointer, so no hover is drawn. A paired keyboard or trackpad on an iPad may need one. To confirm with the ward team.
  • Assumed: a tablet offers Message and shows the number, because many tablets cannot call. Which messaging app it opens, and whether a tablet that can call should still call, are open. To confirm with IT.
  • Assumed: tapping another shift mid-offer keeps the first as a draft, with no question asked. How long a draft lasts, and whether a nurse can have several, are open. To confirm with a nurse.
  • Assumed: the on-screen keyboard leaves the note field and Send offer in view on a tablet. Only the decline sheet is drawn with a keyboard up. To confirm on a device.
  • Assumed: rotating a tablet mid-task keeps the open item, any typed text, and any open sheet. To confirm with engineering.
  • Not confirmed: Android’s sheet limit of 640. No page read gave the number. To confirm with engineering.
  • Assumed: the action bar under the main pane is reachable when a tablet is propped in landscape or held in two hands. Not tested. To confirm on a device.
  • Decided here, reversible: holds last half a second at least. They are Approve swap after a sheet closes or a request opens, Send offer on a reopened draft, a decline sheet’s buttons as the keyboard moves it, and a commit at 200% text. The length is a stand-in. To confirm on a device.

How this screen adapts

SizeLayoutMain action
PhoneOne step per screen, and the list is its own screenAt the bottom, in reach of one thumb
TabletMy shifts beside the current step, with what happens next under itA bar under the main pane, with the main button at the inline end. A step that leads to a commit stays out of that commit’s slot
Tablet landscapeMy shifts, the step, and what happens next. List tickets fold to two linesA bar under the main pane, with the main button at the inline end. A step that leads to a commit stays out of that commit’s slot

On a phone the action bar is drawn at the end of the content, because a review page hides nothing. In the product it is pinned to the bottom of the screen.

The list stays tappable during an offer: see Switching shift during an offer. On a phone, going back reaches the same list, with the same Draft mark. Done on a tablet closes the open pane and leaves the list. At 200% text a tablet changes layout, drawn on Large text.
