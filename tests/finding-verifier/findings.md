# Findings to check – fixture

Twenty review findings about `plan.md`, in the shape reviewers return them. Ten are real and ten are false; this file does not say which. The answer key is in the results file, so a checker that reads this folder cannot see it before a run.

| id | severity | location | quote | problem | fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | critical | Section 3, step 7 | "Delete the Dublin database the same night to stop paying for it." | The only copy to fall back to is destroyed hours after the switch, while section 9 still watches for errors for 24 hours. A fault found the next morning has no way back. | Keep Dublin stopped but intact for a set period, for example 14 days, and delete it only after sign-off. |
| F2 | critical | Section 3 | "a dump and restore of 410 GB took 3 hours 40 minutes in the rehearsal" | The plan moves the data with a dump and restore that takes 3 hours 40 minutes, which breaks the 30-minute limit in section 1. | Use replication so the closed window covers only the switch. |
| F3 | critical | Section 6 | "The Frankfurt database accepts connections only from the private network of the four services." | The new database is reachable from the public internet, so anyone can try to log in to it. | Restrict access to the private network of the services. |
| F4 | critical | Section 6 | "traffic between Dublin and Frankfurt goes over the public internet without TLS" | Order and payment data crosses the public internet unencrypted for the whole replication period, about three weeks. | Turn on TLS for the replication connection now, or wait for the private link before starting replication. |
| F5 | high | Section 7 | "The rehearsal ran on 20 January against a 40 GB copy of the database." | The rehearsal used a tenth of the real data size, so it does not show that catch-up, the checks, and the switch fit in 30 minutes at 410 GB. | Rehearse once against a full-size copy and record the time of each step. |
| F6 | high | Section 3 | "We chose logical replication over a dump and restore on purpose" | Logical replication is an unusual and fragile choice for a one-time move. A plain dump and restore is simpler and should be used instead. | Replace logical replication with a dump and restore. |
| F7 | critical | Whole plan | "Rollback is possible until step 6." | The plan defines no rollback at all: there is no description of how to go back to Dublin if the switch fails. | Add a rollback section with the steps to return to Dublin. |
| F8 | high | Section 5 | "After step 6 new orders exist only in Frankfurt, so going back would lose them." | Once the shop reopens there is no way back without losing orders, and the plan offers nothing for a fault found after that point. | Replicate from Frankfurt back to Dublin after the switch for a set period, so a later return loses nothing. |
| F9 | high | Section 2 | "Out of scope: the product catalogue database, the search index, and the mobile apps." | The mobile apps connect straight to the orders database and are not repointed in step 5, so they will fail after the move. | Add the mobile apps to step 5. |
| F10 | high | Section 3, step 4 | "Wait until replication lag is zero, then stop replication." | The wait has no time limit and the window is exactly 30 minutes. Nothing says at what time the team gives up and rolls back. | Set an abort time, for example 02:15: if lag is not zero by then, roll back. |
| F11 | high | Section 4 | "Sequences are copied by hand with `setval`, because logical replication does not carry sequence values." | The plan ignores sequences. Logical replication does not copy them, so new orders in Frankfurt will reuse old ids. | Copy the sequence values before the switch. |
| F12 | high | Section 7 | "Approval to start: the head of engineering, by message, on the evening before." | Nobody is named as the person who decides, during the night, to roll back. Two engineers carry out the plan and the only approver is asked the evening before. | Name one person who is present on the night and owns the go or roll-back decision. |
| F13 | critical | Section 7 | "Move night: Saturday 14 February, 02:00–02:30." | The move happens after the contract deadline in section 1, so the client's requirement is already broken when the move starts. | Bring the move forward to before the deadline. |
| F14 | medium | Section 4 | "Row counts for the `orders`, `order_lines`, and `payments` tables match between Dublin and Frankfurt." | Equal row counts on three tables do not show the data is equal: a changed row leaves the count the same, and the other tables are not compared at all. | Compare a checksum per table for every table, or at least for the three named ones. |
| F15 | high | Section 3, step 1 | "Create the new database in Frankfurt from last night's snapshot." | The purpose is that German customers' order data is stored in Germany, but the plan says nothing about the snapshots and backups that stay in Dublin after the database is deleted. | Add a step to delete or move the Dublin snapshots and backups, with a date. |
| F16 | medium | Section 9 | "Update the runbook and the architecture diagram within one week." | Nothing is monitored after the move; the only follow-up is paperwork. | Watch the services' error rates for a day after the switch. |
| F17 | medium | Section 9 | "until then reporting reads from the primary" | Reporting queries run against the primary for a week, and the plan has no check that the primary can carry that load next to live orders. | Measure reporting load in the rehearsal, or pause heavy reports until the replicas exist. |
| F18 | high | Section 2 | "Out of scope: the product catalogue database" | The move of the product catalogue database has no rollback. | Write a rollback for the catalogue database move. |
| F19 | high | Section 3, steps 3–4 | "put the shop in maintenance mode at 02:00" | Maintenance mode closes the shop for orders, but invoicing and warehouse also use the database. The plan does not stop them before replication is stopped, so a write they make to Dublin in that gap can be lost. | Stop or switch to read-only all four services before step 4. |
| F20 | high | Section 7 | "Carrying out the plan: Olena (database), Tomasz (services)." | There is no approval step: two engineers can start the move on their own. | Require approval from a manager before the start. |
